# Evolution of streaming-video benchmarks

> Read-this-first overview of how streaming-video **evaluation** grew up: from "answer one question about the whole clip" to "decide *when* and *whether* to speak, second by second, under a real wall-clock budget." Sibling artifacts: [[streaming-benchmarks-design-axes]] (the axis table), [[streaming-benchmarks-benchmark-table]] (verified numbers), [[streaming-benchmarks-concept-graph]] (the map).

## The throughline (one paragraph)

Every benchmark in this sub-topic is a reaction to the same original sin: an offline video-QA test lets a model see the entire clip and pick one static answer, so it can pass by *retrieving a single frame* and never has to reason causally about time. [[streamingbench]] fixed the input side — feed only the stream **prefix up to a query timestamp** so the same question has different correct answers at different moments — and the whole field inherited that "timestamp is a first-class variable" move. From there the work fans along four questions that get progressively harder. **What temporal evidence?** ([[ovo-bench]], [[streameqa]]) split tasks by *where the clue lives* — past, present, or future — turning "forward-active responding" into its own regime. **What interaction shape?** ([[svbench]], [[river-bench]]) reframed streaming as multi-turn dialogue and memory-over-time. **When to speak?** — the proactive turn — began as an add-on scored by a firing tolerance and matured into timing-aware curve metrics (PAUC, [[proactivevideoqa]]) and, in 2026, into the realization that scoring *when* honestly requires the model to **own its own clock** ([[streamingeval]], [[streaming-video-wild]]). And **which modalities?** — the omni line ([[omnimmi]] → [[omnipro]], [[omni-duplexeval]], [[omniinteract]]) — insisted that real streams carry audio and that the model, not the harness, must detect the trigger. The consistent verdict across all of them: humans sit near-ceiling while the best models trail by 25–50 points, and the gap is widest exactly on the timing-and-silence skills that offline evaluation never measured.

## Family A — Evaluator-timed streaming QA (timestamped, passive → contextual)

**Origin — [[streamingbench]] (Nov 2024).** The load-bearing invention is the timestamp-prefix protocol: the model sees only `Video[0:t_q]`, scored by plain MCQ accuracy, so identical questions have different answers at different times. (The protocol itself has a pre-history: [[flash-vstream]]'s VStream-QA had already shipped "answerable only from frames before the timestamp" QA inside a model paper in mid-2024 — StreamingBench's contribution is making it standalone and comprehensive.) It splits streaming competence into three families — Real-Time Visual, Omni-Source (fuse concurrent audio+vision), and Contextual (use prior QA history) — over 18 tasks, and lands the field's founding result: **Human 91.66% vs. best model Gemini 1.5 Pro 67.07%** (a 24.59-pt gap), with a monotone decline 75.69 → 60.22 → 48.73 across the three axes. It also proved the paradox that recurs everywhere: dedicated streaming MLLMs (VideoLLM-online-8B 32.48%, Flash-VStream-7B 24.04%) *underperform* strong offline models on the streaming test, motivating the whole subsequent wave.

![[streamingbench.png]]
*Family A origin: offline asks one question about the whole video; StreamingBench pins each question to a "current time" and splits competence into real-time / omni-source / contextual. Source [[streamingbench]].*

**Refinements.**
- **[[ovbench-videochat-online]] (Jan 2025)** kept the timestamp-anchored MCQ but mined distractors from *other timestamps of the same video* (forcing temporal grounding, not static recognition) and organized 16 subtasks along an explicit Past/Current/Future axis — the split later reused by [[river-bench]] and [[streameqa]]. Crucially it paired the benchmark with a *baseline that ties into memory*: the KV-synced Pyramid Memory Bank (a link into [[streaming-memory]]), scoring 54.9% streaming vs. Flash-VStream 31.2% at smaller size.
- **[[rtv-bench]] (May 2025)** sharpened the "static-retrieval loophole" into **Multi-Timestamp QA**: the *same conceptual question* is re-asked at multiple timestamps with per-timestamp ground truth, so one relevant frame is never enough. It adds gated hierarchical scoring (advanced credit voided unless the group's basics are correct) and a matched online-vs-offline harness. Result: GPT-4o 50.02% overall, with the MTQA split (44.73%) markedly harder than fixed-QA (56.53%) — direct evidence the loophole was real.

**Frontier — [[streamingcot]] (Oct 2025).** Rather than a scoreboard, it contributes the *supervision resource*: 5,000 videos annotated with **time-evolving per-segment answers** (a segment's answer may start indeterminate and refine) plus explicit spatiotemporal chain-of-thought traces grounded to keyframes and GroundingDINO boxes (68,940 segments, 34,470 dynamic QA). It has no model-benchmark table by design — it is the data substrate that lets streaming reasoning be *trained*, feeding the reasoning line ([[thinkstream]]) rather than just measured.

*Influence:* [[rtv-bench]] and [[streamingcot]] both cite the same complaint against [[streamingbench]]/[[ovo-bench]] — snapshot answers under-measure continuous state — and attack it from opposite ends (harder eval vs. richer supervision).

## Family B — Temporal-regime benchmarks (backward / real-time / forward-active)

**Origin — [[ovo-bench]] (Jan 2025).** Where Family A slices by *task semantics*, OVO-Bench slices by **where the evidence lives in time**: Backward Tracing, Real-Time Perception, and — the genuinely new one — **Forward Active Responding**, scored not on accuracy but on *response timing* via dense multiple-triggering with a `2^{-(m'-m)p}` temporal decay. It also introduced the "Unable to answer" option to probe temporal hallucination. The headline: **best model Gemini 1.5 Pro 63.00% vs. Human 92.81%** (~30-pt gap), with the real-time skills STU 58.43% / ACR 66.97% the hardest. This past/present/future framing became the field's default coordinate system.

![[ovo-bench.png]]
*Family B origin: three online-perception regimes (Backward Tracing / Real-Time Perception / Forward Active Responding), each with a timestamped example and a filmstrip of clue/query moments. Source [[ovo-bench]].*

**Refinement / Frontier — [[streameqa]] (Dec 2025).** Takes OVO-Bench's temporal axis and *crosses* it with an orthogonal **embodied-cognition** axis (Perception → Interaction → Planning), yielding 9 categories / 42 sub-tasks over ~21K egocentric QA. It quantifies two penalties that the earlier benchmarks couldn't isolate: an **embodiment penalty** (Qwen3-VL real-time 74% on OVO-Bench → 54.26% here) and a **streaming-input penalty** (EgoGPT offline 62.03% → online 60.93%). Even GPT-5 tops out at 61.3% — situated, forward-looking embodied reasoning is essentially unsolved.

*Influence:* [[streameqa]] explicitly benchmarks against [[ovo-bench]] as its general-streaming baseline and shares the egocentric-proactive sibling relationship with [[egopro-bench]] (Family D).

## Family C — Interactive-dialogue benchmarks

**Origin — [[svbench]] (Feb 2025, ICLR Spotlight).** Reframes streaming from single-shot QA into **temporal multi-turn dialogue**: the unit is a QA *chain* (5–6 context-dependent turns per clip), and chains are joined by typed temporal linkages (Action/Quantity/Person/Object/Event/Environment) into dialogue *paths* across clips. Its second invention is the dual eval regime — a chronological **dialogue walk** vs. a **streaming walk** that jumps (80% probability) to a temporally-linked downstream chain — scored by a GPT-4 judge on five dimensions. GPT-4o leads (Dialogue OS 66.29 / Streaming OS 58.17) below the human ceiling (83.93 / 80.24); the StreamingChat baseline gains +28.79% dialogue over its InternVL2 start.

**Frontier — [[river-bench]] (Mar 2026).** Inherits SVBench's interaction framing but reorganizes it on the temporal axis borrowed from [[ovo-bench]]/[[ovbench-videochat-online]] (past clue / present clue / future cue) and adds two firsts: a **forgetting curve** as a first-class measurement (retro-memory bucketed short → very-long: GPT-4o 63.26 → 52.21 MC as horizon grows) and **asymmetric proactive scoring** (hard-zero for early false alarms, linear decay for late). Its companion long-short-term-memory adapter cuts decay ~12% and lifts a fine-tuned VideoLLM-Online +11.28% on proactive response — a concrete bridge into [[streaming-memory]].

![[river-bench.png]]
*Family C frontier: the four interaction subclasses on a timeline — Retro-Memory (past clue), Live-Perception (present), Pro-Response (must wait for a future cue, then fire fast). Source [[river-bench]].*

## Family D — Proactive-timing + model-owned-timing protocols (the 2026 validity correction)

This family is where "when to speak" stops being a scoring footnote and becomes the whole point — and then where the field notices its own methodology was cheating. How much of a footnote it started as is quantifiable: [[streamingbench]]'s Proactive Output task was ±4s polling with a 2s tolerance, and only **50 of its 250 created PO questions were scored** in the release. The metric ancestry also predates the family: [[sdqes]] (Oct 2024) had already defined latency-aware onset scoring (SR@k with an asymmetric anticipation/latency window) for language-queried event detection — every forward-active decay scheme below is a descendant of that idea.

**Origin of the timing metric — [[proactivevideoqa]] (Jul 2025).** Fixes the question at `t=0` and forces the model to *autonomously choose* when to answer, then scores with **PAUC** — the normalized area under a (time, LLM-judge-score) step curve, per reply turn. A 0.5 floor at the span start encodes "silence beats a wrong early answer," and a timeliness weight `ω` contracts timestamps toward the span start. It empirically shows timing-aware (`ω=0.5`) aligns with human preference better than timing-blind (`ω=1`) — e.g. VAD Cohen κ 0.31/0.36 → 0.45/0.49. It also exposed a failure mode that later validity work would explain: proactive models spam duplicates (MMDuet up to 99.4% duplicate-turn proportion). Two caveats that bound PAUC's authority: it is the one benchmark in the sub-topic where **humans lose** — the human baseline (38.6/38.2/47.0/53.6 across domains) trails GPT-4.1 on three of four, an artifact of the pause-and-type interface rather than model skill — and its debut table was gameable (MMDuet's duplicate spam inflates area). Yet the metric outran the benchmark: within a year [[mmduet2]] adopted PAUC directly as its GRPO training reward — the field's fastest benchmark-metric-to-training-signal loop.

![[proactivevideoqa.png]]
*Family D timing metric: a PAUC worked example — over span [311,360]s the model's four responses trace a step curve; PAUC = area / ideal-area = 0.49. Source [[proactivevideoqa]].*

**Refinements — gaze and persona as the "why now."**
- **[[streamgaze]] (Dec 2025)** conditions past/present/proactive QA on human **eye-gaze scanpaths**, adding a proactive metric that penalizes premature/excessive alerts. Human oracle 0.827 vs. best model GPT-4o 0.535; ViSpeak-7B 0.467 is the strongest streaming model, and VideoLLM-online collapses to 0.080. Its gaze-encoding ablation is sobering: prompting with gaze as text or overlay actively *hurts* (no-gaze 0.446 → 0.429) and salience maps barely help (0.454) — models can't yet use the human attention signal they're being conditioned on.
- **[[egopro-bench]] (May 2026)** makes the proactive trigger **personalized**: a persona + persistent memory is injected so the *same* video demands different help, scored on two orthogonal branches — objective timing (F1/mIoU/GHA, Hungarian-matched) and LLM-judged personalized intent. Its ProAct-Stream model discovers "short thinking": RL-with-think *helps* object alerting (F1 91.19) but *hurts* action-timing (79.66 vs. 85.03), so reasoning must be length-budgeted not to stall the stream — a direct link into [[proactive-response]].

**Frontier — the model must own its clock.**
- **[[streamingeval]] (Mar 2026)** is the methodological reckoning. It argues that offline "pseudo-streaming" scoring silently hands the model free time and unbounded memory, so accuracy tables hide deployability. It runs *every* model — online and offline — through one asynchronous Frame-Player / FIFO-Memory / Responder pipeline with a **strict time-causality, byte-level memory budget**, and folds MaxFPS, accuracy, TTFT, and memory into a single tunable **StreamingScore**. The punchlines: an offline Qwen3-VL-8B tops raw accuracy, but VideoChatOnline-4B sustains only **0.14 FPS** (can't keep pace with a 1-FPS stream), and boosting Flash-VStream's accuracy (OVO 33.15 → 50.31) *collapses* its StreamingScore (2.34 → 0.74) as MaxFPS drops 8 → 1 — the accuracy-vs-deployability trade-off made visible. It also re-confirms StreamingBench's founding irony *under a fair wall-clock protocol*, 16 months later: offline Qwen3-VL-8B beats the best native-online model (StreamForest-7B, OVO 55.57) on accuracy **and** StreamingScore (2.21 vs 1.26); native-online models win only on Forward Active Responding and speed — the premise that streaming needs streaming-native architectures remains unproven on the benchmarks built to prove it.
- **[[streaming-video-wild]] (Jun 2026)** carries the model-owned-timing principle into an *in-the-wild* benchmark: reframes streaming as a per-second **speak-or-stay-silent** decision (two control tokens), scores it with **SW-F1** (windowed TP/FP/FN, `w_FP=0.2` so always-respond spam is penalized but correctness stays primary), and ships a matched harness (three-tier 12h memory + prefix-aware KV cache) proving `<1s` TTFT is achievable over a 2-hour video. Its StreamingHarness-8B reaches 45.8 avg SW-F1 vs. GPT 5.4's 18.3 (~2.5×), and it beats Gemini 3.1 Pro on narration win-rate — the first strong signal that a purpose-built streaming model can *win* on an honest streaming metric.

![[streamingeval.png]]
*Family D validity correction: one unified pipeline (Frame Player / Memory Updater / Responder) forces online and offline models to share a byte-level budget and wall-clock latency, exposing deployment failures invisible in accuracy-only tables. Source [[streamingeval]].*

*Influence:* both 2026 frontier papers name [[streamingbench]]/[[ovo-bench]]/[[svbench]] as "correctness-only, single-turn" predecessors; [[streaming-video-wild]] traces its speak/silence surface directly to the [[videollm-online]] / [[mmduet]] modeling lineage in [[proactive-response]].

## Family E — Omni-modal interaction benchmarks

**Origin — [[omnimmi]] (Mar 2025, CVPR).** The first benchmark to insist that a real stream is *audio-visual* and that interaction is *duplex*. Queries arrive as both text and synthetic audio; six tasks split into streaming comprehension (Action Prediction / Dynamic State Grounding / Multi-Turn Dependency) and proactive control (Proactive Alerting / Proactive Turn-Taking / Speaker Identification), with **silence-as-correct** scoring for turn-taking. Near-floor comprehension (best state-grounding 16.33%) and the fact that reactive video-LLMs simply score ✗ on the proactive tasks motivated the M4 duplex baseline.

![[omnimmi.png]]
*Family E origin: the six-task omni protocol — streaming understanding (answers evolve, later turns depend on earlier) plus proactive reasoning (fire at the right moment, stay silent on noise), each query delivered as text and audio. Source [[omnimmi]].*

**Frontier — three May–Jun 2026 papers each closing a different omni gap.**
- **[[omnipro]] (May 2026)** makes audio *load-bearing* (84% of 2,700 samples need speech or sound) and decouples what-to-say from when-to-say via a **dual probe-vs-online** protocol (Probe Accuracy on content, Online F1 on autonomous timing). Full audio-visual beats video-only by +2.4 to +11.1 F1; best online F1 is only 20.9% (MiniCPM-o 4.5).
- **[[omni-duplexeval]] (May 2026)** builds a timestamp-aware **LLM-as-a-Judge** that grades content and timing jointly (`S = 0.5·content + 0.5·temporal`) over Real-Time Description and Proactive Reminder, using multi-window sampling to tolerate ~2s latency. It quantifies the when-to-respond chasm: best model 20.0 on Proactive Reminder vs. Human-Duplex 92.8, and diagnoses opposite failure modes — LiveCC over-*fires* (91.1% wrong), MMDuet2 over-*silences* (75.8% no-answer).
- **[[omniinteract]] (May 2026)** is the strictest: it ships the **raw** stream with embedded spoken queries and event sounds — timing is *never* given, the model must self-detect the trigger — and grades with a slot-based joint quality-timeliness-flow F1 (IA-QTF1) plus nested-query resumption. Everything is near-broken: best all-slot IA-QTF1 0.368, continuous 1QnA monitoring 0.052, and nested resumption fails 119/120 for Gemini — the honest lower bound for today's omni models.

**The rank-flip diagnosis.** The three May-2026 benchmarks produce three *different* leaderboards: MiniCPM-o 4.5 tops [[omni-duplexeval]] (39.6) and [[omniinteract]] (0.368) but scores only 25.8% in [[omnipro]]'s probe mode vs Gemini-3-Flash's 40.4% — while flipping back to best (20.9%) in OmniPro's *online* mode. The probe-vs-online split inside one benchmark is the clue: **content ability and timing ability are anti-correlated across current models** (explicit-query handlers collapse on proactive slots — Gemini 0.553 real-time vs 0.121 proactive on [[omniinteract]] — while patient monitors lag on probed content), so which benchmark you pick picks which half of the skill you reward.

*Influence:* the omni line is one lineage — [[omnipro]], [[omni-duplexeval]], and [[omniinteract]] all cite [[omnimmi]] as their closest sibling and [[proactivevideoqa]] as the proactive-timing precursor they inherit; they converge with Family D on the same "model must own its timing" principle from the modality side.

## Convergence & cross-pollination

Four independent starting points are collapsing into one shared design:

1. **A common temporal coordinate system.** OVO-Bench's backward/real-time/forward regimes ([[ovo-bench]]) and OVBench's Past/Current/Future split ([[ovbench-videochat-online]]) became the de-facto axes reused by [[river-bench]], [[streameqa]], [[streamgaze]], and [[streaming-video-wild]]. Cross-family benchmarks now report per-regime, not just aggregate.

2. **Timing metrics converged on windowed, asymmetric, area-under-curve scoring.** PAUC ([[proactivevideoqa]]), the `2^{-(m'-m)p}` decay ([[ovo-bench]]), asymmetric hard-zero-early / decay-late ([[river-bench]]), IA-QTF1 time-decay ([[omniinteract]]), and SW-F1's `w_FP=0.2` ([[streaming-video-wild]]) are variations on one idea: **early-and-correct wins, silence beats a wrong alarm, spam is penalized.** [[omni-duplexeval]]'s multi-window sampling and StreamingBench's ±2s tolerance are the same latency-forgiveness mechanism at different granularities. But the convergence is on *shape*, not *values*: the asymmetries encode contradictory beliefs about false-alarm cost — PAUC's 0.5 floor says silence beats a wrong answer, [[river-bench]] hard-zeros any early fire, SW-F1's `w_FP=0.2` deliberately makes spam cheap to keep correctness primary. Since the model population is split into over-firers (LiveCC 91.1%, StreamingVLM 96.7% wrong-fire) and over-silencers (MMDuet2 75.8%, MiniCPM-o 4.5 49.2% no-answer — [[omni-duplexeval]]), **the metric choice effectively picks which failure pole wins**.

3. **The 2026 validity correction reframes the whole enterprise.** [[streamingeval]] and [[streaming-video-wild]] argue that *how* you run the model (wall clock, bounded memory, model-owned timing) changes the ranking as much as the questions do — an evaluation-methodology insight that retroactively qualifies every accuracy-only score in Families A–C. And two measurements now locate *where* the streaming difficulty lives: when the protocol only changes input order, the penalty is tiny ([[streameqa]]: EgoGPT drops ~1.1 pts offline→online), but when the model must own trigger detection, quality roughly halves ([[omniinteract]]: MiniCPM-o 0.683 → 0.348). **Stream ingestion is nearly free; self-triggering is the cliff** — benchmarks that hand the model its query time overstate readiness.

4. **Deep links into the sibling sub-topics.** These are real, not decorative:
   - **→ [[streaming-memory]]:** OVBench's Pyramid Memory Bank ([[ovbench-videochat-online]]), RIVER's LTM adapter ([[river-bench]]), and StreamingHarness's three-tier + prefix-cache design ([[streaming-video-wild]]) are memory contributions shipped *inside* benchmark papers; [[streamingeval]] evaluates [[rekv]]/[[flash-vstream]]/[[streamforest]] under a fixed byte budget — the benchmark family and the memory family measure the same bottleneck.
   - **→ [[proactive-response]]:** the "when to speak" question is operationalized here but the *models* live there — [[egopro-bench]]'s ProAct-Stream, and the [[videollm-online]]/[[mmduet]] speak-silence lineage that [[streaming-video-wild]] extends. [[proactivevideoqa]] is the direct handshake between the two sub-topics (its PAUC is now [[mmduet2]]'s RL reward); strong models on these benchmarks ([[vispeak]], [[streammind]], [[livecc]]) are proactive-response systems.
   - **Half the eval surface lives outside this sub-topic**, inside model papers: VStream-QA ([[flash-vstream]]), EgoSDQES ([[sdqes]]), MAGQA ([[mmduet]]), RealStreamEval ([[evostreaming]]), OmniStar ([[livestar]]/[[livestarpro]]), ODV-Bench ([[streamforest]]), StreamBench ([[streamchat-mem]]), the Live Gaming benchmark ([[proact-vl]]). A synthesis that reads only standalone benchmark papers under-counts the eval landscape by roughly half — and the [[streaming-memory]] sub-topic reports OVO-Bench/StreamingBench deltas as its primary evidence even though neither isolates memory from perception or timing; [[river-bench]]'s forgetting curve is the first purpose-built memory instrument.

## Which numbers to trust (fragility audit)

The scores above are not all equally solid; three structural weaknesses run through the family:

- **Annotation circularity is escalating.** The QA supply chain went GPT-4o auto-captioning ([[streamingbench]], 2024) → Gemini-1.5 semi-automatic timestamps + GPT-4o QA ([[ovo-bench]]) → GPT-5 meta-info extraction ([[streameqa]]) → GPT 5.4 + Claude Opus 4.6 as annotators with Claude Sonnet 4.6 as judge ([[streaming-video-wild]], 2026). Frontier models increasingly both *write* and *take* the tests — [[omnipro]]'s Gemini-3-Flash judge shares a family with its top-scoring probe model — and LLM-judge version drift makes open-ended numbers non-reproducible; only MCQ-accuracy benchmarks are judge-free.
- **The benchmark-ships-a-model pattern is near-universal**, and the companion model tends to top its own benchmark: OVBench+VideoChat-Online, OmniMMI+M4, SVBench+StreamingChat, RIVER+LTM-adapter, EgoPro+ProAct-Stream, Streaming-Eval+StreamingHarness. The most circular headline is [[streaming-video-wild]]'s 45.8-vs-18.3 — own metric, own benchmark, model trained on matched data. Co-design bias is structural, not incidental.
- **Timing conclusions rest on small-n slices.** StreamingBench scored 50 of 250 proactive questions; [[omni-duplexeval]] has 660 short videos and its judge calibration is weakest exactly on its headline axis (Spearman ~0.8 temporal vs >0.9 content); [[streaming-video-wild]] has 138 videos / 100 narration samples; [[streamgaze]] 285 videos — versus the 4,500-QA content slices the field's accuracy claims rest on.

What survives the audit — the multiply-corroborated findings: the ~25–30-pt human gap on prefix-QA (four independent constructions), near-floor proactive-timing performance (four constructions), and offline-beats-streaming-native (found in 2024 and re-found in 2026 under the opposite protocol assumption).

## Where the field is going (grounded in the 2026 frontier)

- **Model-owned timing becomes the default, not an option.** [[streamingeval]] and [[streaming-video-wild]] have made the case that a benchmark without a wall clock and a memory budget is measuring a fiction. Expect accuracy-only streaming leaderboards to be deprecated in favor of composite acc-latency-throughput-memory scores. The obvious unmerged step: [[streamingeval]]'s resource-honest protocol and the omni-duplex timing grading ([[omni-duplexeval]], [[omniinteract]]) are orthogonal today — a model can top the duplex benchmarks while being undeployable, and top StreamingScore while never speaking unprompted. Nobody has run the timing benchmarks *under* the wall-clock protocol yet.
- **The metric is the contribution.** The frontier papers spend their novelty on *scoring* (SW-F1, IA-QTF1, GHA, StreamingScore) rather than more videos — a sign the field now agrees on *what* to test and is fighting over *how to score when and whether*. Given the contradictory false-alarm asymmetries (Convergence §2), the highest-value missing artifact is a **meta-evaluation**: which of the ~8 timing metrics agree on model ranking, and which track human preference — [[proactivevideoqa]]'s ω-ablation Cohen-κ analysis is the seed of that study.
- **Omni + raw stream + self-triggering is the hard frontier.** [[omniinteract]]'s near-zero continuous-monitoring and nested-resumption numbers, and [[omnipro]]'s finding that non-speech-sound triggers are the bottleneck (15.3–22.3 F1), mark the concrete next targets — models that keep a query alive, resume after interruption, and fire on a whistle they had to notice themselves.
- **Personalization and embodiment raise the ceiling.** [[egopro-bench]] (persona-conditioned help) and [[streameqa]] (embodied Perception→Interaction→Planning) show the same clip can demand different, situated responses — the next axis after "when" is "for whom and toward what goal." These are still single-paper axes (as are gaze, the forgetting curve, CoT supervision, and wall-clock resource scoring) — fragile monopolies awaiting replication. And the horizon is short everywhere: [[river-bench]]'s "very-long" recall bucket caps at 3,600 s, so no benchmark yet stresses the day-scale memory the [[streaming-memory]] sub-topic is building for.
- **The gap is not closing yet — it is widening where it matters.** Content-style human gaps have hovered at ~18–30 points since 2024, but the timing gaps measured in 2026 are far larger ([[omni-duplexeval]]: 20.0 vs. 92.8 proactive; [[omniinteract]]: 0.368 best; [[streamgaze]]: 0.535 vs. 0.827) — as benchmarks moved from *what* to *when*, the measured gap grew, because the benchmarks got harder faster than the models got better. Streaming-video understanding as an *interaction* problem — not a perception problem — remains substantially unsolved, which is exactly why this benchmark family is proliferating.
