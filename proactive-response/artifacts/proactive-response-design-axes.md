# Proactive-response — design-axis comparison

The proactive-response problem is: watching a live stream, **decide when to speak (and stay silent otherwise)**, and — jointly — **what to say**. Every system below is one answer to "where does the fire-vs-silent decision physically live, and what quantity does it threshold?" This artifact is the decision matrix across the sub-topic, grouped by the five idea-families used throughout (see [[evolution-of-proactive-response]] and [[proactive-response-concept-graph]]).

The axes were chosen because they are the real forks a builder faces here:

- **Decision locus** — where the timing decision is computed: an *in-band token* the LM decodes, a *separate head/module*, a *decoding-time signal* with no trained head, or a *cascade/agentic* controller.
- **Trigger signal** — the concrete quantity that crosses a threshold (EOS/state-token probability, an informative/relevance/readiness head, perplexity drift, similarity/derivative, visual-change, retrieval score, RL policy).
- **Decision states** — binary speak/silent vs richer structure (ternary, FSM, span, multi-horizon, cascade).
- **Trigger training-free?** — whether the *timing mechanism specifically* needs frame-level supervision/finetuning (the model may still be trained for content).
- **Backbone** — the base perception/LM stack.
- **Streaming data/benchmark introduced** — the corpus or eval it ships (proxy for "who had to build their own labels").
- **Perception ⟂ generation decoupled?** — does a cheap always-on path make the decision while a heavy generator runs (or doesn't) separately? This is the throughput axis.
- **Omni-audio?** — does the trigger consume audio, not just vision (+ASR text)?

Hero figures below are the crux mechanism diagrams for one representative system per family.

## Family A — In-band token gating (EOS/action/state tokens learned into the LM)

The origin thread: fold the timing decision into next-token prediction, so "speak vs silent" is just another token the LM emits. Cheap to add, but the full LM usually runs per frame and the threshold is a hand-tuned knob.

## Family B — Dedicated decision heads / trigger policies (a separate module decides)

Peel the timing decision off the LM head into a purpose-built classifier/module. Buys a cleaner training target and (often) a cheap always-on path, at the cost of an extra component and its own threshold.

![[streammind.png]]
> **Event-gated cognition (StreamMind).** A ~56M Mamba EPFE compresses each frame to one perception token at constant cost; a 4-layer Cognition Gate emits `<response>`/`<silence>` and invokes the full LLM only on fire — decoupling full-FPS (~100 FPS) perception from generation. Source [[streammind]] (arXiv:2503.06220).

## Family C — Training-free / decoding-time triggers (the signal is already in the model)

The frontier move: derive the trigger from a quantity the frozen model already produces — perplexity drift, similarity derivative, visual redundancy, retrieval score — so **no frame-level trigger labels and no streaming SFT** are needed for timing.

![[timechat-online.png]]
> **Trigger for free (TimeChat-Online).** A training-free Differential Token Drop prunes ~80% of redundant tokens; the same drop-ratio timeline's valleys (scene transitions) double as the proactive trigger, with 20K "unanswerable" samples teaching the model to wait. Source [[timechat-online]] (arXiv:2504.17343).

## Family D — Offline-to-streaming adaptation (the model already perceives; it lacks a policy)

Take a strong offline Video-LLM, freeze it, and bolt on only the timing policy — via a separate activation model, a tiny self-evolved LoRA, or a watch/think split. The bet: perception is solved; only "when" is missing.

![[streambridge.png]]
> **Retrofit, don't retrain (StreamBridge).** A frozen offline Video-LLM is the generator; all streaming logic (interleaved memory buffer, round-decayed compression, a decoupled 0.5B activation model firing on score > α) lives in add-on modules. Source [[streambridge]] (arXiv:2505.05467).

## Family E — Agentic / interaction-infrastructure systems

Frame the stream as an agent making structured decisions, or fix an orthogonal infrastructure gap (decoding-time staleness). Mechanisms vary — note that several here still use in-band tokens or separate heads under the hood.

## Reading the axes — what the matrix exposes

1. **The in-band token never dies — it just gets re-pitched.** The origin idea ([[videollm-online]]) recurs across every era: the "in-band token" locus shows up in the RL reframings ([[mmduet2]], [[streampro]]), the ternary/state-token variants ([[streamo]], [[videollm-eyewo]]), the reasoning-loop variant ([[thinkstream]]), and even the "adaptation" family ([[evostreaming]]). Family boundaries here track *what problem a paper claims to solve* far more than *how it decides* — the mechanism space is smaller than the taxonomy suggests. The ablations sharpen this: the token-vs-head interface choice is largely cosmetic, and the **training signal is what's load-bearing**. [[mmduet2]] turned [[mmduet]]'s heads into generated text and the gain came from the GRPO/PAUC reward (WEB PAUC 53.3/4.2% dup vs MMDuet's 38.9 riding 81.3% duplicate spam); [[vispeak]]'s LM-head trigger (60.91 SB) nearly matches its dedicated head (62.00) while *non-joint* training collapses to 34.8–36.0; [[streamo]]'s same 3-state token swings OVO forward-active REC 6.45→27.94 purely on focal-vs-CE loss. The two genuinely non-cosmetic interface choices both encode the same fact — **speak decisions are temporally structured, not i.i.d. per frame**: [[stride]]'s span diffusion (TVG activation-F1 62.8 vs its own AR-head control 35.7) and [[lyrav-dont-pause]]'s stateful Continuing state (removing FDTC: SS −34.4%, NF −55.4%). Nobody else models that structure.

2. **A whole family now gets timing for free.** Family C ([[livestar]], [[livestarpro]], [[lyrav-dont-pause]], [[response-g1]], [[timechat-online]], [[querystream]], and partly [[em-garde]]) reads the trigger off a quantity the frozen model already emits — perplexity drift, similarity derivative, visual-redundancy valleys, retrieval score. This is the direct answer to Families A/B's two costs: hand-tuned thresholds and frame-level trigger labels. The trade is that the signal is a proxy (change ≠ relevance), which is exactly the gap [[querystream]] and [[response-g1]] target by making the trigger *query-relevance*-conditioned rather than *change*-conditioned. Zoom out and there are really **three trigger religions that have never fought on the same turf**: *change*-conditioned ([[timechat-online]] drop-ratio valleys), *relevance*-conditioned ([[em-garde]], [[response-g1]], [[querystream]], [[sdqes]], [[streamov]]), and *uncertainty*-conditioned ([[livestar]]/[[livestarpro]]/[[lyrav-dont-pause]] PPL drift — fire when the current caption stops explaining the frame). [[em-garde]] quietly synthesizes two of them (its θ=0.04 is a derivative *of relevance*). But the PPL family is evaluated almost only on narration (OmniStar/Ego4D) and the relevance family almost only on QA (OVO-FAR/SB-PO) — no paper has run a PPL gate on FAR or a relevance gate on narration, so the religions' relative merits are genuinely unknown.

3. **Decoupling perception from generation is the throughput axis, and it cross-cuts families.** Every "Yes" in the ⟂ column ([[streammind]] ~100 FPS, [[dispider]], [[streamagent]], [[stride]], [[streambridge]], [[streamov]], [[sdqes]], [[egospeak]], [[timechat-online]]) separates a cheap always-on decision path from the heavy generator. The pure in-band-token models (most of Family A) run the full LM per frame and pay for it in FPS. This is the strongest predictor of real-time capability — stronger than which family a paper belongs to. There is also a subtler half of the coupling problem that only four papers acknowledge: **generation blocks perception** — the LIVE lineage implicitly assumes decoding is instantaneous. [[dispider]] (async reaction tokens that never re-enter the decision), [[streammind]] (gate keeps perception running), [[streamchat-nvidia]] (refresh the visual context at every decode step), and [[lyrav-dont-pause]] (sub-budget token emission under a hard Δt=1/FPS cap) treat it; LyraV quantifies the cost of ignoring it — LiveStar truncates to 78.93% Sync Rate vs its 98.29%. And nobody combines [[streamchat-nvidia]]'s within-answer refresh with a trigger: today a trigger fires, then the answer goes stale while decoding.

4. **Binary speak/silent is being abandoned as too coarse.** Ternary states appear for distinct reasons — [[streamo]]'s `Standby` (detected ≠ answer-ready), [[videollm-eyewo]]'s `ask_high` (uncertainty → probe higher-res), [[egospeak]]'s background/other/target (onset conditions on who holds the floor) — alongside FSM ([[lyrav-dont-pause]]'s Continuing-State for speak-while-watching), span-structured ([[stride]]), multi-horizon ([[streamagent]]), and cascade ([[r3-streaming]]). The richer state is almost always about *timing precision*, not content. Note this is convergent evolution with **different semantics nobody has unified**: Standby = event-seen-answer-not-ready, Continuing = still-mid-utterance, ask_high = need better perception, Escalate ([[r3-streaming]]) = need deeper reasoning, speculative ([[streamagent]]) = hunt future evidence. The union — a ~6-way action space {silent, standby, respond, keep-talking, probe-perception, escalate-reasoning} — is unbuilt, each paper having added exactly one extra state. The threshold zoo argues the same way from below: [[assistpda]] needs θ=0.96 vs 0.7 *across its own two tasks* — a single global threshold cannot serve multiple proactive behaviors, which is the real case for both threshold-free generation ([[mmduet2]]) and richer states.

5. **RL is the current cure for the threshold knob — but its value-add over frozen retrieval is unproven.** [[mmduet2]], [[thinkstream]], [[streampro]], [[em-garde]] (parser only), and [[r3-streaming]]'s router all replace a hand-tuned informative/EOS/readiness threshold with a critic-free GRPO policy trained under a timing-aware reward (PAUC, turn-F1, RLVR, TB-GRPO band-anchoring). The shared insight is that all of them use **coarse span-level rewards precisely to dodge frame-precise labels** — exact reply timestamps are unannotatable at scale. The internal evidence is strong ([[mmduet2]] SFT-only 19.59 → RL 34.69 SB-PO; [[r3-streaming]]'s vanilla-GRPO router collapses to 100% escalation vs TB-GRPO's stable 24%), but zero-training [[response-g1]] beats or matches the whole RL wave on the shared metrics (FAR 58.2, SB-PO 44.0) — so whether RL adds anything over a strong frozen backbone + retrieval has not been established anywhere.

6. **Everyone builds their own labels — trigger supervision is the bottleneck.** The "data/benchmark" column is almost entirely bespoke corpora (VAPDA-127K, PROASSIST, Stream-IT, Streamo-465K, OmniStar(Pro), ViSpeak-Instruct, ESTP-Bench, ProReady-QA, Live Gaming Benchmark, StreamPro-Bench, WAT-85K…). There is no shared streaming-trigger training set; each paper reformats offline annotations into frame-level speak/silent labels. The training-free family exists partly *because* those labels are so expensive.

7. **One universal pathology, many private cures, zero head-to-head.** Whatever the labels, they are savagely imbalanced — silence:response is ~310:1 on Ego4D and 71:1 on SoccerNet per [[streammind]] — so every supervised trigger must fix it, and each picked a different fix: plain CE with w≈1–2 ([[videollm-online]], whose ablation explicitly finds focal/OHEM *unnecessary* — CE best), weighted CE W≈10P ([[streammind]]), focal γ=2 + frequency-α ([[streamo]]), class-balanced effective-number β=0.9999 ([[streampro]]), negative-frame subsampling ρ=0.1 ([[proassist]] — narration F1 30.1→58.7, the single biggest timing lever in this table), transition-weighted BCE γ=5 ([[proact-vl]]), w_pos=N_neg/N_pos ([[roma]]). The only within-paper comparison, [[streampro]]'s mini-ablation (CE 6.6 < focal 14.2 < CB 16.3 on its bench), **directly contradicts** [[videollm-online]]'s "plain CE suffices". A controlled loss bake-off on one backbone/benchmark does not exist — a cheap paper that would settle a live contradiction.

8. **Omni-audio is still the exception — but "visual" proactivity has its own blind spot.** Only [[egospeak]], [[roma]], [[vispeak]], and [[streamov]] consume audio+video; the mainstream triggers on vision alone (with [[livecc]] using ASR *text*, not the audio signal). For a problem literally about "when to speak," the human-natural cue — the audio turn-taking channel — is underexploited outside these four. The caveat cuts both ways: on [[egospeak]]'s Ego4D conversational split, audio-only (69.2 mAP) matches audio+video (69.0) — for turn-taking, vision adds nothing there (A+V does win on EasyCom) — and [[vispeak]]'s humor subtask is its worst score, so subtle visual-*social* triggers remain unsolved even by purpose-built systems. The visual-trigger premise holds cleanly mainly in task/anomaly domains ([[assistpda]], gesture/anomaly cues).

9. **Anticipation is missing from every LLM trigger.** The non-LLM primitives fire *ahead* of the moment: [[egospeak]] predicts speech onset over a future ~2 s window (its motivating gap: silence-threshold detection gives ~200 ms of reaction vs the 600–1500 ms real turn-taking uses), [[sdqes]] scores a 5 s anticipation tolerance, and [[assistpda]] reports 29.19 s average advance time for anomaly *prediction* (against an LLM-decided oracle onset). Every LLM-based trigger in Families A–D is **at-onset reactive** — it fires when the evidence is already visible; [[streamagent]]'s "speculative" horizon is the lone LLM-side gesture at this and it is an agentic plan, not a trained onset forecaster. Porting egospeak-style future-window supervision onto a VLM decision token is an obvious, unbuilt hybrid.

10. **The unbuilt combination.** No system unites all three of the winning traits at once: a **training-free** decoding-time trigger (Family C), a **decoupled constant-cost** perception path (the throughput trick of [[streammind]]/[[stride]]), and **omni-audio** turn-taking. The closest partials are [[timechat-online]] (training-free + decoupled, vision-only) and [[streamov]] (decoupled + omni, but a trained head). A training-free, full-FPS, audio-aware trigger is the open corner of this matrix. A second convergence the matrix predicts: the trigger is merging with the memory system's salience statistic — [[timechat-online]]'s drop ratio is compression signal *and* trigger, [[livestarpro]]'s PPL is timing gate *and* memory-salience score, [[thinkstream]] keeps reasoning-KV as memory anchors, [[r3-streaming]] fuses memory→respond→reason into one cascade — so "when to speak" may end up a free readout of whatever the memory system already computes.

11. **Comparability is better than it looks — and worse.** There *is* one clean shared ladder: StreamingBench Proactive Output, with a seven-rung history — [[videollm-online]] 3.92 → [[dispider]] 25.34 → [[mmduet]] 29.44 → [[mmduet2]] 34.69 → [[em-garde]] 38.0 → [[stride]] 42.8 → [[response-g1]] 44.0 — and its top rungs are all training-free or frozen-responder systems while the fully-trained lineage plateaus ~35. But two confounds gut naive readings. First, **backbone generation**: the top two ride Qwen3-VL-8B, and [[stride]] discloses its base already scores 32.40 PO untouched. Second, **metric gaming and fragmentation**: [[mmduet]] hits PAUC 46.0 on EGO via 99.4% duplicate replies — above [[mmduet2]]'s honest 33.6 — and "OVO-Bench FAR" currently denotes three incompatible numbers (standard accuracy: [[response-g1]] 58.2; [[em-garde]]'s own online-F1 rescoring: 30.99; [[streampro]]'s own F1: 20.6) that must never be compared across columns. Timing-precision metrics beyond that scatter across TimeDiff, Temporal F-Score, PAUC, ARS, ESTP-F1, SR@k — which is what still hides how far apart these designs really are.
