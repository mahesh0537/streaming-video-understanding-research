# The Evolution of Proactive Response

*Read-this-first overview of how "when should a streaming video model speak on its own?" became a field. Sibling artifacts: [[proactive-response-design-axes]] (the design table), [[proactive-response-benchmark-table]] (verified evals), [[proactive-response-concept-graph]] (the concept map). Hub: [[proactive-response]].*

## The throughline

Proactive response is the problem of a model that watches a live stream and decides, causally and by itself, **when** to speak and **what** to say — no user turn to react to. The whole field descends from one move in [[videollm-online]] (Jun 2024): fold the timing decision into the language model as a per-frame streaming-EOS token, so "stay silent" and "start replying" become ordinary next-token predictions. Everything since is a fight over **where the timing decision should live**. Four answers crystallised. (A) Keep it *in-band* — a token the LM emits, refined into action tokens, three-state tokens, and eventually RL'd rewards. (B) Pull it *out* into a dedicated decision head or trigger policy running beside a frozen generator, so perception never blocks on decoding. (C) Make it *training-free* at decode time — read perplexity drift, visual change, or query-relevance and fire without ever learning a timing label. (D) *Retrofit* offline video-LLMs that already perceive well but lack any policy, using memory buffers and tiny self-generated data. A fifth strand (E) builds the surrounding *agentic infrastructure* — omni-modal evidence routing, budget-controlled fast/slow reasoning, decode-time visual refresh. The 2026 frontier is a convergence: the timing decision is increasingly trained with **RL under timing-aware rewards** ([[mmduet2]], [[streampro]], [[thinkstream]]), gated by **query-relevant evidence** rather than raw visual change ([[streamov]], [[response-g1]], [[querystream]]), and installed with **near-zero new data** on frozen backbones ([[evostreaming]]) — while a parallel push fixes the benchmarks that were quietly leaking the timing decision to the evaluator.

## Family A — In-band token gating (the timing decision *is* a token the LM emits)

**Origin.** [[videollm-online]] (LIVE) turned proactivity into a learned per-frame choice with its streaming-EOS loss — LM loss on reply tokens plus an EOS loss on silent frames, where EOS is a supervision target that is never stored in context so long-stream cost stays bounded. It set the streaming-dialogue baseline (LM-PPL 2.43 / TimeDiff 2.32s / Fluency 42.6% on Ego4D at 13.5 FPS) that every later family measures itself against [[videollm-online]].

**Refinements.** The token got richer and the training signal got smarter. [[stream-vlm-qevd]] made timing a decodable *action* — `<next>` (observe another frame) vs `<feedback>` (start speaking) — and showed removing those tokens collapses Temporal F-Score from 0.56 to a turn-based 0.50 while fluency is untouched, proving the token, not the language head, carries the timing skill. [[livecc]] learned the same silence/speak boundary from free web ASR by densely interleaving timestamped words with frames, with an ellipsis token on silent frames. [[proassist]] adapted VideoLLM-Online with a synthetic 30,135-dialogue egocentric corpus and negative-frame subsampling (NFS, ρ=0.1) to fight the speak/silent imbalance — its ablation shows the fix is load-bearing (narration F1 30.1→58.7). That imbalance (silence:response reaches 310:1 on Ego4D per [[streammind]]) is the family's shared unsolved subroutine: every paper reinvents a fix — weighted CE with an empirically-tuned ≈10P silence weight ([[streammind]]), focal loss ([[streamo]]), NFS ([[proassist]]), effective-number class-balancing ([[streampro]]), transition-weighted BCE ([[proact-vl]]) — and [[streampro]]'s loss ablation (CE 6.6 < focal 14.2 < CB-Stream 16.3 on its bench) is the *only* within-paper comparison of any of them. [[assistpda]] and [[lion-fs]] carried the EOS formulation into new regimes — surveillance anomaly prediction (per-task EOS thresholds) and a fast/slow split where cheap per-frame response-determination runs at 4× the frame rate of expensive keyframe generation. [[videollm-eyewo]] widened the vocabulary to a ternary {silence, response, ask_high} so the model can *probe* for a high-res frame when uncertain.

**Frontier.** Two upgrades dominate. First, **more decision states**: [[streamo]] folds a three-state token (Silence / Standby / Response) into next-token prediction, using focal loss to survive >80% silence, and its state-token design lifts OVO-Bench forward-active tasks sharply (Streamo-7B OVO 55.61, +13.83% over Dispider's 41.78; the focal-vs-CE ablation moves REC/SSR/CRR from 6.45/20.99/41.67 to 27.94/50.72/82.5). Worth noting: across the whole family, the vocabulary differences (EOS vs `<next>` vs ellipsis vs NO REPLY) are largely cosmetic — Standby is the one state with genuinely new semantics ("event seen ≠ answer ready"), and it gets independently reinvented as [[streamready]]'s answer-readiness score and [[r3-streaming]]'s Respond gate. Second, **RL on the timing decision itself**: [[mmduet2]] recasts speak-vs-silent as text-to-text "reply or NO REPLY" generation — the logical endpoint of the vocabulary line, no special token at all — and trains it with GRPO under a timing-aware PAUC reward, dissolving MMDuet's hand-tuned threshold and cutting duplicate-spam (ProactiveVideoQA PAUC 53.3 at 4.2% dup vs MMDuet 38.9 at 81.3%). [[thinkstream]] fuses the two — a per-step `<response>`/`<silent>` contract wrapped in chain-of-thought, trained with a verifiable RLVR reward whose explicit timing term rewards firing near ground truth. Its CoT doubles as memory, and the ablation is striking: keeping reasoning KV beats no memory (64.8 vs 56.9) while discrete captions actively *hurt* (48.7) — reasoning traces are a better streaming memory than captions.

## Family B — Dedicated decision heads / trigger policies (a separate module decides)

**Origin.** [[mmduet]] reframed interaction as a **video-text duet** where the video is a turn-taking participant, and replaced the single EOS token with **two per-frame binary heads** (informative + relevance) on a frozen-ish LLaVA-OneVision-7B, plus a "remove previous response" trick to kill caption repetition. It beat the single-token baseline decisively on StreamingBench Proactive Output (29.44 vs VideoLLM-Online 3.92). In parallel, [[sdqes]] gave the family its problem statement — proactive reaction *is* language-queried streaming onset detection — with asymmetric-tolerance, first-k metrics (SR@k, SMD@k) that bake precision into the score so spurious early fires are penalised.

**Refinements.** The head got decoupled from generation and specialised. [[dispider]] split the system into three async modules (light perception, a compact decision LLM with a binary head on a ⟨TODO⟩ token, a heavier reaction LLM) whose decision input excludes reaction-generated tokens — perception never blocks on decoding; it became the standard proactive baseline (StreamingBench 53.12). [[egospeak]] specialised the head to egocentric turn-taking, anticipating speech onset ~2s ahead with a three-class output so onset conditions on whether someone else holds the floor. [[streammind]] made the head cheap enough to run at every frame: a Cognition Gate (the LLM's first 4 shallow layers) reading one SSM perception token per frame, hitting ~100 FPS while only invoking the full LLM on events (TriggerAcc 43.34% vs VideoLLM-Online 32.34%). [[vispeak]] pushed into purely-visual triggers (respond to a gesture or anomaly with no text query) using a two-stream chat template plus a binary informative head; ablating the joint head drops StreamingBench overall from 62.00 to ~35. [[roma]] moved the head to the omni (audio+video) setting with a 2-layer speak-head parallel to the LM head; ablating it collapses proactive scores to near-zero.

**Frontier.** Trigger policies are getting structurally richer and better-supervised. [[proact-vl]] adds a learned per-second `<FLAG>` gate with transition-weighted BCE, calibrated to a human speaking rate, for real-time AI companions. [[streamready]] gates a frozen LLM on a `<RDY>` token over a hierarchical memory tree and introduces an Answer-Readiness-Score metric so timing is scored separately from content (its timing gap over baselines far exceeds the raw-accuracy gap). [[stride]] abandons per-frame binary decisions altogether, modelling the trigger as a **span-structured masked-diffusion** window denoised in K steps for temporal coherence (ET-Bench TVG activation-F1 62.8 vs a per-frame AR head's 35.7). [[streamagent]] turns the head into an **anticipatory planner** that forecasts *when and where* future evidence will appear across reactive/proactive/speculative horizons and hunts it with tools — the bridge into Family E.

## Family C — Training-free / decoding-time triggers (no timing label learned)

**Origin.** [[timechat-online]] noticed that its Differential-Token-Drop signal — built to prune ~80% of redundant streaming tokens — *already* localises scene transitions: the valleys in the per-frame drop ratio are exactly the moments worth responding at, so proactive timing came **for free** with no trigger head and no timing labels. The trigger-for-free pattern generalises to any streaming-compression statistic: in the sibling [[streaming-memory]] sub-topic, [[fluxmem]] reuses the backward-similarity scores its memory-write step already computes as a zero-overhead proactive trigger. And most of this family is one idea in different clothes — change-point detection on a cheap surprise statistic, whether that statistic is a drop ratio, a caption's perplexity, or a similarity derivative.

**Refinements.** The decode-time signal generalised beyond visual change. [[livestar]] rejected the EOS/silence token outright, gating on a **perplexity-verification** test — speak iff the ongoing caption's PPL under the new frame jumps above α× its previous value — one interpretable knob, zero timing training (OmniStar SemCor 3.19 vs MMDuet 1.93). [[response-g1]] made the decision explicit and *query-conditioned*: generate query-guided scene graphs online, store them, retrieve the top-K query-relevant graphs, and fire on relevance — all on a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B (OVO-Bench FAR 58.2 vs StreamAgent 45.4; retrieval is the main lift, PO 36.8→44.0).

**Frontier.** Two 2026 moves refine the training-free gate. [[lyrav-dont-pause]] wraps a frozen LiveStar backbone in a three-state (Triggered/Continuing/Silent) FSM plus a tiny learned token-pacer, so the model emits sub-budget token chunks *every* frame and never pauses perception to decode a full sentence — turning gating into **synchrony** (Sync Rate 98.29% vs LiveStar 78.93%; the Continuing State is the load-bearing novelty). [[em-garde]] moves the heavy semantic parsing out of the loop entirely: a 7B parser runs *once* at query time to emit textual visual cues, then a training-free 2B embedder fires on the *derivative* of text-video similarity at ~13 FPS (OVO-FAR F1 30.99 vs MMDuet-2 20.51). [[querystream]] (ICLR 2026, compact) pushes the relevance idea end-to-end — Query-Aware Differential Pruning + Relevance-Triggered Active Response — explicitly rejecting the "visual change = important" heuristic that [[timechat-online]] popularised.

## Family D — Offline-to-streaming adaptation (the models perceive; they lack a policy)

**Origin / thread.** The premise: a strong offline VideoLLM already contains the world knowledge to be a good streaming assistant — what it lacks is a *timing policy*. [[streambridge]] retrofits any frozen offline model (LLaVA-OV, Qwen2-VL, Oryx) with add-on modules only: a producer-consumer memory buffer, round-decayed token compression under a 16K budget, and a **separate 0.5B activation model** with a binary head + `<ACT>` token that decides when to fire. With its Stream-IT data, Qwen2-VL-7B reaches OVO-Bench-RT 71.30, exceeding GPT-4o (64.46).

**Refinements → frontier.** [[wat]] splits online understanding into query-agnostic "watching" (dual FIFO + redundancy-evicted memory) and query-triggered "thinking" (retrieval with a contrastive alignment loss), so late-arriving questions don't lose transient events (StreamingBench 77.70). [[evostreaming]] is the thread's sharpest statement: with **no architecture change**, the frozen base model plays three roles — data generator, relevance annotator, roll-out policy — to bootstrap ~1,000 of its own causal SILENT/RESPOND trajectories, then LoRA-finetunes on them. This installs the timing policy with **139× less data** than [[timechat-online]] and near-zero offline regression (Qwen2-VL Overall 43.8→54.6, FAR 12.6→30.6; offline drops <2 pts). It also ships **RealStreamEval**, a strict causal protocol with a verbosity penalty — a direct critique that OVO-Bench's prefix-dump and StreamingBench's per-second polling both leak the timing decision to the evaluator.

## Family E — Agentic / interaction-infrastructure systems

**Origin.** [[streamchat-nvidia]] tackled a subtler proactivity bug — *decoding-time* staleness — by refreshing the visual context from a FIFO frame queue at **every decode step** via weight-shared cross-attention and a parallel 3D-RoPE that gives text and visual tokens a shared clock, so a long answer stays aligned with the still-evolving stream.

**Frontier.** The 2026 systems wrap the timing decision in richer agent loops. [[streamov]] adds proactive timing to a *frozen* Qwen3-Omni-30B via a ~19M-param cross-attention **trigger head reading prefill hidden states**, fed by a three-way disentangled (visual/audio/AV) evidence-routing memory — the audio-visual analogue of Family C's relevance gating (SOVBench-O 83.8%, Proactive-QA F1 90.5; StreamingBench-Omni +22.5 over [[roma]]). [[r3-streaming]] cascades three agentic decisions per step — Remember (age-aware forgetting, reusing TimeChat-Online's operator), Respond (a readiness gate), Reason (budget-controlled fast/slow routing trained by TB-GRPO) — hitting OVO-Bench 57.92 / StreamingBench 76.36 while dropping 95–96% of visual tokens. [[streampro]] reframes the whole eval axis: "proactive" should mean *agency under partial observation*, not reactive see-then-answer; its class-balanced SFT + multi-grained GRPO (turn-F1 + trajectory-rubric rewards) lifts StreamPro-Bench proactive F1 to 41.5 vs the prior best open-source 10.4 — roughly 4×.

## Convergence & cross-pollination

Five convergences define where the families are merging:

- **RL is eating the timing head.** The learned-threshold heads of Family B and the in-band tokens of Family A are both being replaced by **RL under timing-aware rewards**: PAUC in [[mmduet2]], RLVR in [[thinkstream]], TB-GRPO routing in [[r3-streaming]], multi-grained GRPO in [[streampro]]. The reward function (PAUC / turn-F1 / trajectory rubric) is now where the design action is, not the head architecture. But the hand-tuned threshold never dies — it *migrates* into the reward: [[mmduet2]]'s four term weights, [[streampro]]'s deliberately loose reward tolerance (τ=8 in the reward vs strict multiplicative eval scoring beats a tight τ=3, 28.1 vs 25.8), and [[r3-streaming]]'s escalation band (η=0.3, γ=0.2), without which vanilla GRPO collapses to always-escalate at 100% where TB-GRPO holds 24%.

- **Change-triggers → relevance-triggers.** Family C started with visual-change signals ([[timechat-online]]) but the frontier fires on **query-relevant evidence**: scene-graph retrieval in [[response-g1]], evidence routing in [[streamov]], and the explicit change-vs-relevance rejection in [[querystream]]. This is the deepest link into the sibling [[streaming-memory]] sub-topic — evidence-guided triggering and bounded query-aware memory are becoming the same mechanism (see [[streamov]], [[wat]], [[streamready]] hierarchical memory trees; [[livestarpro]] carries the perplexity gate of [[livestar]] into a tree-structured long-horizon memory, an explicit bridge documented in [[evolution-of-streaming-memory]]).

- **Frozen backbones win.** [[streambridge]], [[evostreaming]], [[response-g1]], [[streamov]], and [[lyrav-dont-pause]] all keep the generator frozen and train only a tiny policy/head — a decisive shift from the full-model fine-tunes of 2024–25, motivated by preserving offline ability (all report <2-pt offline regression). The trajectory across Family B is heads shrinking as backbones freeze — from MMDuet's dual heads on a fine-tuned LLM to [[streamov]]'s ~19M cross-attention over frozen prefill states — converging on **MLLM-as-trigger**: read the frozen model's own hidden states rather than retrain the model. A frozen trigger also rides every backbone upgrade for free.

- **Timing supervision is collapsing toward zero cost.** The label source evolved from human real-time narration ([[videollm-online]] on Ego4D) → LLM-synthesized dialogue ([[proassist]], 30,135 dialogues over 479h written by a 70B LLM) → free web ASR timestamps ([[livecc]]) → the model's *own* trajectories ([[evostreaming]], ~1,000 self-generated roll-outs, 139× less data) — with the training-free Family C as the limit point where the timing label vanishes entirely.

- **The benchmark reckoning.** The families now share a critique of their own evaluators. [[evostreaming]]'s RealStreamEval, [[streampro]]'s StreamPro-Bench, [[streamready]]'s Answer-Readiness-Score, and [[mmduet2]]'s PAUC all argue that prefix-dump / per-second-polling protocols leak the timing decision and reward duplicate spam. The smoking gun is [[mmduet2]]'s autopsy of its own predecessor: MMDuet's high egocentric PAUC (46.0) was achieved with **99.4% duplicate replies** — reply-spam maximises area-under-curve metrics, so the metric gaming was load-bearing for the old numbers. This is the tightest thread into the sibling [[streaming-benchmarks]] sub-topic — proactive eval is being rebuilt around causal, verbosity-penalised, timing-aware scoring (see [[evolution-of-streaming-benchmarks]]). [[sdqes]]'s latency-aware SR@k/SMD@k was the early template the frontier is now generalising.

## Tensions the numbers expose

Reading results *across* papers surfaces frictions no single paper states:

- **Training-free keeps beating trained — with a confound nobody controls.** Frozen, prompt-only [[response-g1]] (Qwen3-VL-8B) posts OVO FAR 58.2 (vs trained [[streamagent]] 45.4) and StreamingBench PO 44.0 (vs [[mmduet2]]-RL 34.69, [[dispider]] 25.34). Either the trained trigger heads of 2024–25 were under-trained, or backbone generational gains dwarf every method delta. Nobody runs the controlled comparison — same backbone, trained head vs prompted gate — so whether the timing decision needs training at all is empirically open.
- **FAR numbers are incommensurable.** The same OVO-Bench "Forward Active Responding" name hides at least three scoring conventions: [[streampro]] reports Streamo at F1 5.4 and MMDuet2-3B at 6.5; [[em-garde]] reports MMDuet-2 at F1 20.51 under its own online recall/precision protocol; [[response-g1]] reports 58.2 accuracy-style; [[stride]] reports Qwen3-VL-8B 46.30→59.70. Any cross-paper FAR table without protocol footnotes is fiction — see [[proactive-response-benchmark-table]] for the caveated numbers.
- **Small + RL beats big + architecture.** [[thinkstream]]-3B hits OVO overall 59.66, above [[r3-streaming]]'s 7B/4B cascade (57.92) and Streamo-7B (55.61) — cutting against the modular-decoupling premise that good timing needs dedicated machinery rather than a well-rewarded policy.
- **Does vision even matter for timing?** [[egospeak]]'s Ego4D ablation: audio-only 69.2 mAP ≥ audio+vision 69.0. An awkward datum for a field premised on vision-driven triggering — and untested in the LLM lineage, since the omni systems ([[roma]], [[streamov]], [[vispeak]]) add audio but never ablate timing-from-audio-alone.
- **The anticipation gap.** The pre-LLM formulations built *anticipation* in — [[egospeak]] predicts speech onset ~2s ahead; [[sdqes]] scores an anticipation window — but the LLM family almost universally fires reactively at frame *t*. Only [[assistpda]] (anomaly *prediction*, ~29s average advance time) and [[streamagent]]'s speculative horizon carry forecasting forward; no general video-LLM predicts future speak-points.
- **Open-ended format collapses the baselines.** On [[streamo]]'s bench, all baselines score 0 mIoU on open-ended forward grounding (Streamo-7B: 29.4) once multiple-choice options are removed — likely task-format fit, not a capability gap; a caution for the 2026 pattern of pairing each system with a home benchmark and a bespoke LLM judge.

## Where the field is going (grounded in the 2026 frontier)

Three trajectories are clear from the 2026 papers. **First, timing becomes an RL problem with a contested reward.** [[mmduet2]], [[thinkstream]], [[streampro]], and [[r3-streaming]] all train the speak decision with GRPO/RLVR variants; the open question is the reward — PAUC, turn-F1, trajectory-rubric, and band-anchored escalation ratios are competing formulations, and the winner will define "proactive" for the next generation. **Second, the trigger fuses with memory and becomes relevance-conditioned.** [[streamov]]'s evidence routing, [[response-g1]]'s retrieved scene graphs, [[querystream]]'s relevance gate, and [[livestarpro]]'s perplexity-scored hierarchical tree point to a single mechanism where deciding *when* to speak and deciding *what to remember* are one query-aware retrieval — collapsing the boundary between this sub-topic and [[streaming-memory]]. **Third, the evaluation substrate is being rebuilt.** Nearly every 2026 flagship ships its own benchmark *and* a critique of the old ones (RealStreamEval, StreamPro-Bench, OmniStarPro, SOVBench, the Live Gaming Benchmark), all converging on strictly-causal, verbosity-penalised, timing-separated scoring. The near-term frontier is therefore less about new architectures and more about **frozen backbones + tiny self-generated data + timing-aware RL rewards + honest causal benchmarks** — with the omni-modal ([[streamov]], [[roma]]) and synchrony ([[lyrav-dont-pause]]) settings as the hardest remaining regimes, where models must decide when to speak *while still perceiving*, in real time, from audio and vision at once. Two post-trigger gaps stand out: [[lyrav-dont-pause]] owns pacing/synchrony and [[streamchat-nvidia]] owns decode-time visual refresh — the two halves of "keep watching while speaking" — yet no system combines them; and duplex interruption is benchmarked ([[omnimmi]], [[omni-duplexeval]]) but built only once ([[vispeak]]'s Interruption task). Once *when to start* is solved, *what happens while speaking* — pacing, refresh, being interrupted — is the next field.
